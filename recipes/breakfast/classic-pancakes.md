# Classic Pancakes

**Category:** breakfast  
**Prep time:** 10 minutes  
**Cook time:** 20 minutes  
**Servings:** 4 (about 12 pancakes)  
**Difficulty:** Easy  

> Light, fluffy, and golden — the kind of pancakes that make a weekend morning worth waking up for.

---

## Ingredients

- 1½ cups (190 g) all-purpose flour
- 2 tbsp granulated sugar
- 1 tsp baking powder
- ½ tsp baking soda
- ¼ tsp salt
- 1¼ cups (300 ml) buttermilk
- 1 large egg
- 2 tbsp unsalted butter, melted and slightly cooled
- 1 tsp vanilla extract
- Butter or neutral oil for the pan

---

## Instructions

1. In a large bowl, whisk together the flour, sugar, baking powder, baking soda, and salt.
2. In a separate bowl or jug, whisk together the buttermilk, egg, melted butter, and vanilla extract.
3. Pour the wet ingredients into the dry ingredients. Stir gently with a spatula until just combined — a few lumps are fine. Do not overmix or the pancakes will be dense.
4. Let the batter rest for 5 minutes. This gives the baking powder time to activate.
5. Heat a non-stick pan or griddle over medium heat. Add a small knob of butter or a thin film of oil and let it melt.
6. Pour approximately ¼ cup (60 ml) of batter per pancake onto the pan. Cook until bubbles form across the entire surface and the edges look set, about 2–3 minutes.
7. Flip once and cook for another 1–2 minutes until the underside is golden brown.
8. Transfer to a warm plate and repeat with the remaining batter, adding more butter or oil between batches as needed.
9. Serve immediately with maple syrup, fresh fruit, or your preferred toppings.

---

## Notes

- **No buttermilk?** Mix 1¼ cups of regular milk with 1 tbsp of white vinegar or lemon juice. Stir and let it sit for 5 minutes before using.
- **Keep them warm** while you finish the batch by placing cooked pancakes on a baking sheet in an oven at 90°C (200°F).
- **Storage:** Leftover pancakes keep in the fridge for up to 3 days or frozen for 2 months. Reheat in a toaster for best texture.

---

## About This Recipe

This is the recipe I grew up making every Saturday. The buttermilk is the key — it reacts with the baking soda to give the pancakes their rise and a subtle tang that plain milk can't replicate.
