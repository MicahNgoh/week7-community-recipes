# Jollof Rice

**Category:** dinner  
**Prep time:** 20 minutes  
**Cook time:** 50 minutes  
**Servings:** 6  
**Difficulty:** Medium  

> A West African staple — deeply savory, smoky at the edges, and impossible to make just once.

---

## Ingredients

- 2 cups (400 g) long-grain parboiled rice
- 400 g (one 14 oz can) crushed tomatoes
- 2 medium red bell peppers, roughly chopped
- 2 medium tomatoes, roughly chopped
- 1 scotch bonnet pepper (or habanero), seeds removed if you prefer less heat
- 1 large onion, divided — half roughly chopped, half finely sliced
- 4 tbsp neutral oil (sunflower or vegetable)
- 2 cups (480 ml) chicken or vegetable stock
- 1 tsp curry powder
- 1 tsp smoked paprika
- ½ tsp dried thyme
- 1 bay leaf
- Salt to taste

---

## Instructions

1. Add the crushed tomatoes, red bell peppers, fresh tomatoes, scotch bonnet, and the roughly chopped half of the onion to a blender. Blend on high until completely smooth.
2. Heat the oil in a large, heavy-bottomed pot over medium-high heat. Add the finely sliced onion and cook, stirring occasionally, for 5–7 minutes until soft and beginning to caramelize.
3. Pour the blended tomato mixture into the pot. It will splatter — use a lid or splatter guard. Cook uncovered over medium-high heat, stirring every few minutes, for 20–25 minutes until the mixture has reduced by about half and the oil starts to rise to the surface. This step is what builds the flavor — do not rush it.
4. Add the curry powder, smoked paprika, thyme, and bay leaf. Stir well and cook for 2 more minutes.
5. Rinse the rice under cold water until the water runs clear. Add it directly to the pot and stir to coat every grain in the tomato base.
6. Pour in the stock. The liquid should just cover the rice by about 1 cm (½ inch). Season with salt, stir once, and bring to a boil.
7. Once boiling, reduce the heat to the lowest setting, cover tightly with a lid or foil, and cook for 25–30 minutes without lifting the lid.
8. After 25 minutes, check the rice — it should be cooked through and the liquid fully absorbed. If the rice is still firm, add 2–3 tbsp of water, re-cover, and cook for 5 more minutes.
9. Remove the bay leaf. Fluff gently with a fork and serve hot.

---

## Notes

- **The smoky bottom:** A slightly scorched layer at the bottom of the pot (called *party jollof*) is considered a sign of an authentic, well-cooked jollof. High heat in the last 5 minutes develops it deliberately — listen for a gentle crackling sound.
- **Make it a full meal:** Serve alongside fried plantain, grilled chicken, or a simple green salad.
- **Spice level:** One scotch bonnet with seeds removed is mild-to-medium. For a mild version, substitute a mild red chili. For more heat, leave the seeds in.

---

## About This Recipe

Jollof rice is one of those dishes every West African family makes differently, and everyone is convinced their version is the best. This one leans Ghanaian — slightly more tomato-forward than the Nigerian style, with a longer reduction for a deeper flavor.
